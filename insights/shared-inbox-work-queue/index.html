When does a shared inbox need a work queue?

By Adjona Business Applications · Published 26 November 2026

A shared mailbox can show who has written to you. When several people must act on the same request, the harder question is who owes the next reply.

A supporter emails a charity about changing a regular donation. One colleague acknowledges the message. A second needs to check something with finance. On Thursday the supporter rings back, and the colleague answering the phone has to read the entire thread to work out what anyone promised.

That is a useful test for a busy inbox. Could someone covering the desk tell the caller who owns the request, what is outstanding and when to expect an answer?

Try the simplest workable arrangement

Choose one type of enquiry and look through ten recent examples with the people who answered them. Count how many finished with a single reply. For the others, identify the moment responsibility became unclear. Was it when the message arrived, when someone forwarded it, or when the team started waiting for another department?

If most enquiries end with one reply, allocating a person to monitor the mailbox and agreeing how colleagues mark work they have picked up may be enough. Where follow-up regularly crosses teams or shifts, try a small shared register. Give each open request an owner, a next action, a date and a reference back to the correspondence. Keep sensitive details in the approved location.

Test the handover

Take the donation example. "Waiting for finance" leaves the request open indefinitely. "Aisha will chase finance on Wednesday and update the supporter by Thursday" gives the covering colleague a commitment they can act on. Agree who reviews overdue items and who takes over when the owner is away.

After a fortnight, ask how many callbacks still needed a search through old emails. If that number has fallen and staff are keeping the register current, the team has a workable process. If maintaining it means copying the same details into several places, that recurring step is a good candidate for automation. If the register itself outgrows a spreadsheet or list, our guide When does your business need Dynamics 365 instead of Excel, SharePoint or Microsoft Lists? sets out the next options.

For a discussion with Adjona, bring one anonymised thread where responsibility changed hands. It makes a more useful starting point than an estimate of how many messages arrive each day.
