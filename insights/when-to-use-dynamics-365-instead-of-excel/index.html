When does your business need Dynamics 365 instead of Excel, SharePoint or Microsoft Lists?

By Adjona Business Applications · Published 9 November 2026

Not every organisation needs a CRM system. A spreadsheet, a Microsoft List or a simple Power App can serve a small team well for years. The question is not which tool is most capable, but whether the way you manage enquiries and customers has outgrown what you use now, and whether a larger system would justify its cost and upkeep. The same applies to charities that manage paid training, service contracts or partnership enquiries.

When Excel is still enough

Many organisations start with a spreadsheet. Enquiries arrive by email, documents sit in SharePoint or OneDrive, and one person keeps a list of what is in progress. For a small team with a few enquiries a month, that can work perfectly well. A useful enquiry spreadsheet usually records:

  • Organisation and contact details.
  • Date and source of the enquiry.
  • Service requested.
  • Current status.
  • Next action and follow-up date.
  • Estimated value.

If one person maintains it consistently, access is controlled and everyone knows who follows up, there may be no reason to change.

Problems start when several people edit the same file, copies circulate, or nobody can say with confidence which enquiries need attention this week.

The warning sign is not the number of rows. It is the effort needed to keep the information accurate and useful.

When Microsoft Lists and SharePoint make more sense

Microsoft Lists sits between a spreadsheet and a dedicated application. It gives you structured records, shared views, permissions and simple automation, using the Microsoft 365 licences most organisations already have.

A List can track incoming enquiries, show who owns each one and record progress. Power Automate can notify a colleague when an enquiry arrives or remind the owner when a follow-up is overdue. SharePoint holds the related documents, with version history, permissions and retention.

For a straightforward process, this may be all you need.

Lists has limits, though. It is not designed for many related records, such as organisations with several contacts, each linked to opportunities, activities and invoices. It also offers little in the way of structured sales stages, forecasting or detailed security by team. When a process needs those, a growing collection of lists and flows can become harder to maintain than a platform built for the job.

What Dynamics 365 Sales adds

Dynamics 365 Sales is Microsoft's customer relationship management application, built on the Dataverse data platform. It treats each enquiry as part of a connected record of leads, contacts, organisations, opportunities and activities. With it, a team can:

  • Capture incoming leads and decide which are worth pursuing.
  • Link contacts to the organisations they work for.
  • Move opportunities through defined sales stages.
  • Record meetings, tasks and other customer interactions.
  • Assign ownership and see outstanding actions.
  • Report on the pipeline and, depending on the licence, forecast sales.

This matters most when several people contribute to the same sales process, or when managers need a reliable view of likely income.

Software does not create a sales process by itself, though. If nobody has agreed what counts as a qualified enquiry, when an opportunity begins or who owns the next step, Dynamics 365 will hold the same uncertainty in a more expensive place.

Design the process first, then configure the system around it.

Could Power Apps and Dataverse be a better fit?

Not every requirement is about sales. An organisation may need to manage inspections, service requests, approvals, training records or another operational process. A tailored app built with Power Apps may suit that better than a sales application.

Power Apps can store its data in Microsoft Lists or in Dataverse, and the choice affects cost. As a guide, an app built on Microsoft Lists or SharePoint can usually run on the Power Apps rights included with Microsoft 365. An app that stores its data in Dataverse is a premium app, and everyone who uses it needs a premium Power Apps licence or a Dynamics 365 licence that covers it. Dataverse brings stronger relationships between records, finer security and business rules, so the extra cost can be worthwhile, but it should be a deliberate choice.

A tailored app is not automatically cheaper or simpler than an established product. Licensing, data design, security, testing and future maintenance all need weighing. The right answer comes from the process, not from the technology that happens to be available.

When finance integration changes the decision

The picture changes when sales and finance need to share information. Enquiries may be tracked in one place, quotes prepared in another and customer details typed again when an invoice is raised. As volumes grow, that repetition causes errors and delays.

If the organisation already uses Business Central for finance, check its built-in relationship management first. It includes contacts, sales opportunities with stages, recorded interactions and campaigns, which may be enough for a simple sales process without adding Dynamics 365 Sales.

Where a fuller sales application is needed, Dynamics 365 Sales can connect to Business Central through Dataverse. Depending on the configuration, customers, products, sales orders and posted invoices can be synchronised between the two.

Connecting them is more than switching on a link. You need to decide which system owns each type of record, how existing records are matched, which fields move in which direction and what happens when a synchronisation fails. Then the connection needs testing with real examples.

Buying two substantial applications to save a few minutes of re-keying is rarely worthwhile. Where disconnected sales and finance processes cause real delays, errors or reporting problems, integration can pay for itself.

The costs extend beyond licences

Comparing monthly subscription prices is only the start. The full cost of a business application usually includes:

  • Discovery and process design.
  • Preparing, cleaning and moving existing data.
  • Configuration and any customisation.
  • Integration with other systems.
  • Security roles and access management.
  • Testing and training.
  • Ongoing administration, support and changes.

Licensing differs between Microsoft Lists, Power Apps with Dataverse and Dynamics 365, so establish the actual requirements before estimating. A simple system that staff use every day often gives better value than a sophisticated one that few people trust.

Six questions before choosing a CRM or business application

1. What problem are we trying to solve? Name the actual difficulty: missed follow-ups, information entered twice, inconsistent records or reports that take too long.

2. Who needs to use it? Consider who creates records, who updates them, who approves decisions and who needs reports.

3. How connected is the information? A simple enquiry register may work well in Lists. Organisations with several contacts, opportunities and activities each may need Dataverse or Dynamics 365.

4. What should happen automatically? List the notifications, approvals, assignments and integrations that would genuinely save time.

5. What security and records rules apply? Consider confidentiality, permissions, audit trails, retention and any regulatory obligations.

6. Can we justify the full cost? Estimate the time lost and errors corrected today, and the value of better information. Compare that with implementation, licensing and ongoing support.

The answers should point to the technology, not the other way round.

Start with the process, not the product

Excel, Microsoft Lists, Power Apps and Dynamics 365 are not steps every organisation must climb. They suit different kinds of work. A well-designed List can be the right long-term answer for one team, a tailored Power App for another, and Dynamics 365 Sales for an organisation managing a substantial pipeline.

The aim is not the most advanced system. It is a dependable process, accurate information and tools that fit the work people actually do.
