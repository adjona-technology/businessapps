What should a sales process review settle before you buy a CRM?

By Adjona Business Applications · Published 17 December 2026

Bring one enquiry that became a sale and another that went quiet. Together they can show which decisions a new system would actually need to support.

Suppose a director wants a CRM because proposals keep going unanswered. Sales says the problem is chasing replies. Finance says buyers often ask for revised terms after the proposal has been sent. Both may be right. Buying a system around the first explanation could give the team a more efficient way to chase quotations that were incomplete in the first place.

Reconstruct two journeys

For each enquiry, lay out what the customer asked for, what the team promised, where the information was recorded and who picked it up next. Work from anonymised examples. A blank process diagram often shows how people expect the work to happen. A dated trail reveals the adjustments staff make to get a real sale through.

Mark the first point where someone had to guess, retype information or ask a customer for the same detail twice. Estimate how often it happens. Five minutes of rekeying on every order and a two-day delay on an occasional exception call for different decisions.

Leave with decisions somebody can implement

A useful review should settle who owns each handover, what information they need and how the team will know it has improved. For the proposal example, the team might agree that sales confirms the billing contact and any exceptional terms before seeking approval. Finance can then identify omissions before the proposal reaches the buyer.

The resulting brief should distinguish an immediate process change from work requiring configuration or integration. Give each proposed change an owner and a test. "The person covering sales can find every proposal awaiting approval and see who must act" is something staff can demonstrate. "Improve visibility" leaves the supplier and the buyer free to imagine different outcomes.

If January's budget discussion includes a CRM purchase, take those decisions into it. They give you a basis for comparing a change to your current tools with an implementation proposal, including what each option would cost to run and support.
